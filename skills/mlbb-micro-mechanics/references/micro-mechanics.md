# Micro mechanics: hero knowledge, spacing, power spikes, hidden mechanics
Sources: HID (primary), TERM (definitions), EXP / GOLD (power-spike use), BUILD (scaling)
Scope: How to read a hero, choose items by scaling, win lane through last hits and spacing, use power spikes, and the high-level hidden mechanics (animation cancel, basic-attack reset, redirecting a pull skill with a blink, input buffer). Hero names are dropped (abstraction rule); each worked example keeps the mechanism, not the hero. Whether a given hero still behaves this way is a LIVE item.
Data quality: hero names in the notes were whisper spellings and several duels were not identifiable; the mechanisms are reliable, the hero attributions are not.

## 1. Stats versus damage type (Tip 1/4)
- Four separate things: physical attack and magic power are STATS that boost damage; physical damage and magic damage are the damage actually dealt. (src: HID 00:30)
- What decides the damage type is the SKILL, not the item you buy. A hero that buys magic items can still deal physical damage because its skill deals physical damage; scaling can be mixed (physical and magic percentages on one skill). (src: HID 00:30; hero example → L-MICRO-01)
- ITEM RULE: look at how your skills SCALE, not the damage type they deal. IF the scaling text is blue THEN buy magic items; IF red THEN buy physical items. "Read, read and read again" the skill description. (src: HID 01:40; same scaling logic in BUILD 08:30)

## 2. Know your hero (Tip 2/4): the "identification card"
- Micro mechanics = understanding a hero: skills, counters, power spike, scaling, best items, damage types, even hidden mechanics. (src: HID 01:00; TERM 06:48)
- Three levels of mastery, shown as pillars: easy, medium, hardest ("?"). (src: HID 01:00)
  - Easy: gold gap, last hit, itemization (§3).
  - Medium: spacing and power spike (§4–5).
  - High: hidden mechanics (§6).

## 3. Easy level: gold gap, last hit, itemization (Tip 3/4)
- The game is about the gold gap between you and the enemy and how you use that gold. (src: HID 01:10)
- In lane, land the final hit on minions while denying the enemy the same chance. (src: HID 01:10; TERM 08:08)
- Worked situation: a minion is low and the enemy steps up for the last hit → poke immediately, which forces a choice: trade back or secure the last hit. (src: HID 01:25)
- Spend the gold by reading skill scaling (§1). (src: HID 01:40)

## 4. Medium level: spacing
- Reading is not enough at the next level; learn spacing: every hero has a different range; exploit it to hit the enemy without being hit back (the longer range pokes, the other has to close in). (src: HID 02:10)
- Range advantage can depend on a cooldown: a hero may out-range another only while a particular skill is ready; once it is used the extra range disappears during the cooldown and the other hero has the longer reach. (src: HID 02:25; heroes → L-MICRO-02)
- IF you are the shorter-range hero THEN wait until the enemy's long-range skill is spent, then engage. (src: HID 02:40)

## 5. Medium level: power spike
- **Power spike** = when your hero is at its strongest and most impactful. (src: HID 02:55; TERM 09:46)
- What builds a spike (on-screen graph) (src: HID 03:10): skill damage (early), sustain from the passive, core items (the highest point, around the mid game), then falling off as enemies build anti-heal, defence and penetration.
- Example logic: strong early (skill damage + passive sustain), stronger mid game when the core items are done, falls off late against anti-heal, defence and penetration → spike = early to mid game. (src: HID 03:10; hero → L-MICRO-03)
- USE: know when to play aggressively and when safely; compare which hero is ahead at which level. (src: HID 03:10; use in lane: EXP 05:58, GOLD 03:15)
- Worked comparison: at level 2 one hero wins the duel by spamming a skill; from level 4 the other takes over because its ultimate is much stronger (the on-screen split "level 2 vs level 4"). (src: HID 03:25; heroes → L-MICRO-03)
- HOW TO WIN THE DUEL: find the enemy's MAIN SOURCE OF DAMAGE (on screen, the first skill) and play around it (dodge it). (src: HID 03:40)
- A hero whose skills only dash to marks is limited by available targets: deny it marks (keep far from minions) when fighting it. (src: HID 03:55; mechanic dependency → L-MICRO-03)

## 6. High level: hidden mechanics
- Mid-level mechanics come with experience; high-level mechanics are harder and full of hidden mechanics. (src: HID 04:10)

### 6a. Animation cancel
- **Animation cancel** = casting a skill again during the first part of its animation (e.g. mid-air) to skip the rest, so damage comes out faster. Without cancel: jump → land → end; with cancel: jump → end → next cast. The hero that cancels deals more damage in the same time. (src: HID 04:10–04:25; hero → L-MICRO-04)

### 6b. Basic-attack reset
- **Basic-attack reset** = using a skill right after a basic attack to cancel the "pull back" part of the attack animation (aims → shoots → pulls back); the next basic attacks come out faster. (src: HID 04:46–05:03; hero → L-MICRO-04)

### 6c. Redirecting a pull/hook skill with a blink
- After a pull/hook lands, pressing the blink spell moves the hooked target with the hero; the trick works in any direction ("curving" the hook). (src: HID 05:15; hero and spell → L-MICRO-04)

### 6d. Input buffer (Tip 4/4)
- **Input buffer** (not a buff) = a short window in which a pressed ability waits if its activation condition (e.g. a nearby enemy) is not met yet, then fires instantly once met. (src: HID 05:29, 06:12)
- Analogy in the video: open the bottle first, then walk up, so you splash the moment you are close enough. (src: HID 05:42)
- RULE: IF an ability has a condition (e.g. needs a nearby target) THEN press it BEFORE the movement/blink that satisfies the condition, so it fires with no delay. Example: ability-first, then blink to the enemy is faster than blink-first, then ability. (src: HID 06:12–06:25; the video's final rule, hero → L-MICRO-04)

## 7. Summary checklist
1. Read each skill's scaling colour and buy that stat (§1).
2. Know the hero's identification card (skills, counters, spike, scaling, damage types, hidden mechanics) (§2).
3. Win the gold gap: last hit and deny; poke to force a choice (§3).
4. Compare ranges and cooldown-dependent range; wait for the enemy's long-range skill to be spent (§4).
5. Compare power spikes by level; dodge the enemy's main damage skill (§5).
6. Learn animation cancels, resets, redirects and buffer-queuing for your own hero (§6).
