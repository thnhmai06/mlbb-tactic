# Draft: bans, team compositions, pick priority
Sources: DRAFT (primary), TERM (draft terms), GOLD / EXP / JUNG / ROAM / MID (role-specific draft rules, cross-referenced)
Scope: Ban logic for public (blind-ban) games, the four team compositions and their win conditions, the pick-priority list, duo/trio/solo adaptation and per-role hero-pool archetypes. Hero names are dropped (abstraction rule); every comp is described by the role traits it needs. Role-level draft rules live in 03a–03e; this file does not repeat them.
Data quality: DRAFT was extracted from 46 frames only; hero icons on its slides carry no names, and the XP-lane hero-pool audio (15:24–15:44) is garbled.

## 1. Draft vs blind ban, and the MPL caveat
- Do not copy professional-league drafts blindly. The pro format is a turn-based draft ban (ban, pick, ban, pick); public games use a blind ban (everyone bans first, then picks). Different rules mean different strategy. (src: DRAFT 00:43, 00:53; TERM 04:36)
- Pro teams know their opponent days ahead and ban/pick against that specific opponent and its players' signature heroes; that information advantage does not exist in public games. (src: DRAFT 01:03)
- Two comps pro teams often use are valid in public play: dive and front-to-back. (src: DRAFT 01:11, 05:23, 07:45)
- Draft = the phase before the game with two actions: **ban** (prevent everyone from using specific heroes) and **pick** (choose your team's heroes). (src: DRAFT 01:35)

## 2. Two key concepts
- **Team comp**: the kind of lineup the team has (e.g. assassin jungler vs tank jungler, healing roamer vs tanky roamer). (src: DRAFT 01:42)
- **Win condition ("win comp")**: the specific condition the team must fulfil to win. Each comp has its own: how it opens a war, each role's job in a teamfight, even why it targets tanks first or damage dealers first. (src: DRAFT 02:02)
- Understand both before picking heroes. (src: DRAFT 02:30)

## 3. Ban phase
- Public ban phase structure: 3 initial bans, reveal, then 2 final bans, reveal (L-DRAFT-01). (src: DRAFT 02:41; TERM 04:51)
- Goal of banning: make sure YOU have more overpowered (OP) heroes available than the enemy. (src: DRAFT 02:49)
- The video's model: every meta has about three OP heroes (rule of thumb, L-DRAFT-02). (src: DRAFT 02:58)

### 3a. OP-hero rule by pick order
- IF your team has FIRST pick THEN leave only ONE OP hero open after the ban phase (ban only two of the three). BECAUSE if more than one is left the enemy also gets OP heroes, maybe more than you. (src: DRAFT 03:09)
- IF your team has SECOND pick THEN make sure THREE OP heroes are left, so after the enemy takes one you take the other two. (src: DRAFT 03:22)
- Simulation (src: DRAFT 03:33): 1 OP left → first-pick team has the advantage; 2 OP left → even; 3 OP left → second-pick team has the edge (takes two vs one).
- The video calls this "extremely important": it tells you when to ban OP heroes and when to ban normal heroes. (src: DRAFT 04:00)

### 3b. What to ban when not banning OP heroes
- Ban heroes that COUNTER the heroes you plan to use. (src: DRAFT 04:06; example pairs → L-DRAFT-03)
- Also ban heroes that exploit the weakness of your chosen comp (e.g. for dive: heroes that cancel the engage). (src: DRAFT 10:21)
- Respect ban: ban a hero because the enemy player is exceptionally good with it (see 01-glossary). (src: TERM 05:06)
- Bans are wasted if you then pick the wrong heroes; therefore understand the comp and its win condition. (src: DRAFT 04:31)
- Flow: Ban → choose team composition → Pick. After the ban phase you should know your comp; the comp depends on what is left (e.g. IF the OP front-to-back heroes are banned THEN go dive). (src: DRAFT 10:13)

## 4. The four team compositions
None hard-counters another; "it's all about execution". (src: DRAFT 09:54)

### 4a. Dive
- Lineup by role (src: DRAFT 04:44): XP laner that can jump into the enemy backline; assassin or diving jungler; high-mobility mid; roamer that can jump in; agile marksman.
- Goal and win condition: dive the enemy backline in teamfights and eliminate the enemy DAMAGE DEALERS first; whenever a fight breaks out, the enemy damage dealers must go down first. (src: DRAFT 05:09)
- Weakness: heroes that can cancel an engage; ban them (names → L-DRAFT-04). (src: DRAFT 05:27)
- Needs tight coordination: focus ONE target at a time, do not waste skills on the same enemy, use just enough to secure the kill, then move to the next. (src: DRAFT 05:35)

### 4b. Poke (the opposite of dive)
- Structure: 3 poke heroes + 1 executor + 1 tanky disengager who can stop enemy engages. (src: DRAFT 06:03)
- Role constraints: XP laner and jungler may be poke, executor or tank; mid and gold MUST be poke; roamer should be a tank or a poke hero. (src: DRAFT 06:13)
- Goal: chip enemies from long range, then the executor finishes them when HP is low. Win condition: the enemy must already be LOW HP before the fight starts. (src: DRAFT 06:24, 06:32)
- Risk: IF the enemy dives first THEN your tank must cancel their engage immediately. (src: DRAFT 06:38)
- Hero traits: poke heroes need spammable long-range skills; executors need skills that reset on a kill; the disengager is a tanky hero that can stop engages. (src: DRAFT 06:44, 07:00, 07:18)
- Rank caveat: works well below the top-rank tier because most players there do not know how to counter it; in higher ranks players understand vision and flanking, so it falls off (L-DRAFT-05). (src: DRAFT 07:18, 07:25)

### 4c. Front-to-back
- Works in every rank; the most common comp (casuals, boosters and pro teams). (src: DRAFT 07:32)
- Two parts (src: DRAFT 07:47–08:08):
  - **Backline**: TWO consistent (not burst) damage dealers of DIFFERENT damage types (one physical, one magic): a marksman in gold lane and a DPS mage in mid.
  - **Frontline**: ONE initiator, ONE sustained hero, ONE disengager, filled from XP lane, jungle and roam.
- Win condition: the backline melts the enemy front line while your front line ZONES their back line to keep it out of the fight; usually the XP laner or roamer zones with lots of CC. (src: DRAFT 08:24, 08:33)
- It explains why a pro team focused tanks first: it plays front-to-back and hits the front line first. (src: DRAFT 08:40)
- Weaknesses: over-relies on marksman and mage (if they are picked off the team can do nothing); depends on 5-man fights, so it is weak against split push. (src: DRAFT 08:47)

### 4d. Hypercarry
- Goal: make ONE player a star who carries the team; wins fast. The hypercarry is a gold laner or a jungler. (src: DRAFT 09:04, 09:14)
- Only high-DPS heroes can hypercarry, NOT long-cooldown burst heroes. (src: DRAFT 09:23)
- The roamer always sticks with the hypercarry; the rest of the team must be independent. (src: DRAFT 09:31)
- Win condition: the hypercarry must get rich; IF they fall behind in gold THEN you certainly lose. Risk: reliance on one hero; reward: snowball and end fast. (src: DRAFT 09:38, 09:46)

## 5. Pick priority
- Pick order matters as much as comps. After bans decide the comp; every pick must fit it. (src: DRAFT 10:05)
- Priority list: 1 OP heroes; 2 flexible heroes (can play several lanes); 3 counter-picks; 4 tanks; 5 damage dealers; 6 initiators. (src: DRAFT 10:24)
  - A flexible pick keeps lane assignment open so you can adapt to enemy picks. (src: DRAFT 10:26)
  - Method: go down the list; IF nothing OP or flexible that fits your comp remains THEN counter-picks; then tanks to complete the front line; damage dealers after seeing enemy picks; initiators last. (src: DRAFT 10:56)
- IF you pick a damage dealer THEN choose the damage type your team lacks (e.g. a magic gold laner when the team already has a lot of physical damage). (src: DRAFT 11:26; same rule in EXP 01:39, GOLD 02:30)
- IF the enemy picks a counter to your flexible pick THEN change the flexible hero's lane/role (e.g. move it to a diving roamer role) instead of dropping the comp. (src: DRAFT 11:32)
- Use the comp's requirement list as a checklist while drafting (dive: XP "can dive", roam "can dive", jungle "diving or assassin", mid "agile", gold "agile"). (src: DRAFT 11:00)
- Worked example (src: DRAFT 10:43–10:54): bans leave the OP front-to-back heroes gone → choose dive; last two bans go to the heroes that cover dive's weakness; picks follow the list: strong flexible hero first → the enemy counters it → switch it to a diving roamer → counter-pick a jungler that counters the enemy XP hero and fits dive → tanky diving XP laner for the front line → after enemy picks, a magic gold laner (the team already has physical damage from jungle and XP) and a mid laner.

## 6. Adapting to trio, duo and solo
- Trio: focus on the mid trio (mid, jungle, roam); they dictate rotations and follow the same four comp positions. (src: DRAFT 12:14)
- Duo: depends on teammates (paired with another duo, a trio, or three solos). IF paired with a trio THEN follow their draft direction and fit their comp. Otherwise focus on three synergies. (src: DRAFT 12:25–12:42)
- **Synergy 1: roam + gold** (src: DRAFT 12:47–13:30)
  - Check damage types: IF the team already has two physical heroes THEN the gold laner picks magic.
  - IF you are the roamer THEN check your front line; IF you have no tank THEN pick one, unless your roam + gold combo covers it.
  - Make the roamer cover the gold laner's weakness (e.g. a high-damage gold laner with no mobility is stuck once slowed → pair a roamer with CC immunity and a trap skill) and vice versa (a roamer whose hook is easy to dodge → pair a gold laner with a CC skill that makes it land).
- **Synergy 2: mid + jungle** (the video calls it the strongest duo) (src: DRAFT 13:51–14:12)
  - Two rules: complement each other; pay attention to their behaviour.
  - IF your jungler desperately needs to farm THEN do not pick a farm-heavy mage, and vice versa. IF your mage needs to farm a lot THEN pick a jungler that can PRESSURE the enemy mid.
- **Synergy 3: jungle + roam** works similarly to mid + jungle. (src: DRAFT 14:21)
- **Solo rules** ("lower your expectations"): the team should have (1) both a frontline and a backline, (2) both physical and magic damage, (3) both early- and late-game heroes, (4) someone who can engage and someone who can disengage. IF matched with a trio THEN follow their composition. (src: DRAFT 14:27, 14:49)

## 7. Hero-pool mastery per role (archetype slots, not names)
Master a small pool of archetypes per role (src: DRAFT 14:54):
| Role | Slots |
|---|---|
| Roam | initiator tank, dive tank, support |
| Jungle | tank jungler, magic assassin, physical assassin |
| XP lane | sustain, damage, tank (⚠ unverified: audio garbled, frame labels used) |
| Gold lane | late-game carry, strong laner, magic marksman |
| Mid lane | late-game mage, burst mage, utility mage |
- Above all, know every hero's skills and passives so you can counter the enemy's gameplay. (src: DRAFT 15:54)

## 8. Cross-references to role-level draft rules
- XP lane: count tanks in jungle + roam; tanky XP laner picks first, damage XP laner picks late (03a §1–2). (src: EXP 00:40–01:55)
- Gold lane: pick by team comp in a party; rank-dependent solo rules; "1 vs 9" (03b §1). (src: GOLD 00:55–02:36)
- Jungle: tank vs damage jungler by tank count, then comp (03d §2). (src: JUNG 00:25–01:22)
- Roam: tank roamer if fewer than 2 tanks; heal vs damage by meta (03e §2). (src: ROAM 00:30–01:05)
- Mid: mage type by enemy tankiness and team magic damage (03c §1). (src: MID 00:27–01:43)
- Common rule across roles: tank count in the team drives which role takes the tank; damage type must be balanced between physical and magic. (src: DRAFT 11:26, EXP 01:39, GOLD 02:30)
