# I Studied Pro Player Draft So You Don't Have To
Source: I Studied Pro Player Draft So You Don't Have To.mp4 | ~16:15 | Draft guide for public (ranked) games: ban logic by pick order, 4 team comps with win conditions, pick-priority list, duo/trio/solo synergy rules, how many heroes to master per role.

## Summary
- Public ranked draft is a BLIND ban (all bans first, then picks), unlike MPL's turn-based draft ban; do not copy MPL drafts blindly (MPL teams scout opponents for days).
- Ban logic: aim to have more OP heroes available than the enemy. Number of OP heroes you leave open depends on whether you have first or second pick. When not banning OP heroes, ban counters to the heroes you plan to play and to your comp's weakness.
- Choose ONE of 4 team comps (dive, poke, front-to-back, hypercarry) after the ban phase; each has its own win condition. Every pick must fit the chosen comp.
- Pick priority: OP > flexible > counter-picks > tanks > damage dealers > initiators (frame list).
- Duo/trio/solo adaptation: trio focuses on mid/jungle/roam; duos use roam+gold, mid+jungle, jungle+roam synergies; solo needs 4 balance rules.

## Tactical knowledge

### Draft vs. blind ban, and MPL caveat
- [00:43] [T] Do not copy MPL drafts without thinking: MPL uses a turn-based "draft ban" system (ban, pick, ban, pick); public games use a "blind ban" (everyone bans first, then picks). Different rules mean a different strategy.
- [00:53] [T] Blind ban = ban everything first, then pick your heroes.
- [01:03] [T] MPL teams know their opponent days ahead, so bans/picks are targeted at that opponent (video's example: a team facing a certain player always bans that player's signature hero). That information advantage does not exist in public games.
- [01:11] [T] Still, there are two team comps that MPL teams often use and that are valid for public play (dive and front-to-back, see below; the video also says MPL teams use the dive comp at 05:23 and front-to-back at 07:45).
- [01:35] [T] Draft = phase before the game with two actions: Ban (prevent everyone from using specific heroes) and Pick (choose the heroes your team will play).

### Two key concepts
- [01:42] [T] Team comp (team composition) = what kind of lineup the team has (e.g. assassin jungler vs tank jungler, healing roamer vs tanky roamer).
- [02:02] [T] Win comp (win condition) = the specific condition your team must fulfill to win. Each team comp has its own win condition: how the team opens a war, each role's job in a teamfight, even why a team targets tanks first instead of damage dealers.
- [02:30] [T] Understand both concepts before picking heroes.

### Ban phase structure (public)
- [02:41] [T] Ban phase = 3 initial bans, reveal the bans, then 2 final bans, reveal again (frame: 5 bans per side, 3 then 2).
- [02:49] [T] Goal of banning: make sure YOU have more OP heroes available than the enemy.
- [02:58] [T] In every meta there are usually about 3 OP heroes (the "3 OP" assumption is the basis of the rule below).

### OP-hero ban rule by pick order
- [03:09] [T] If your team has FIRST pick: after the ban phase only ONE OP hero should be left. If more than one is left, the enemy also gets OP heroes, maybe more than you. So with first pick ban only 2 of the 3 OP heroes, not all three.
- [03:22] [T] If your team has SECOND pick: make sure THREE OP heroes are left, so after the enemy picks one you take the other two.
- [03:33] [T] Simulation (frames "1 OP hero left", "2 OP hero left", "3 OP hero left"):
  - 1 OP left: first-pick team has the advantage (gets the OP hero, the other side does not).
  - 2 OP left: even (each team gets one).
  - 3 OP left: second-pick team has the edge (takes two while first team gets one).
- [04:00] [T] This is "extremely important": it tells you when to ban OP heroes and when to ban normal heroes instead.

### What to ban when not banning OP heroes
- [04:06] [T] Ban heroes that COUNTER the heroes you plan to use (the specific counter pairings are in the LIVE table).
- [04:31] [T] Your bans are wasted if you then pick the wrong heroes; so to draft properly you must understand team comp and its win condition.
- [10:21] [T] Also ban the heroes that exploit the weaknesses of your chosen comp (e.g. for dive: the heroes that cancel engage/gauge).
- [10:13] [T] After the ban phase you should already know which comp you are going for; every hero you pick must fit that comp. Flow shown on screen: Ban -> Choosing team composition -> Pick. The comp choice depends on what is left after the bans (e.g. if the OP front-to-back heroes are banned, go for dive).

### Comp 1: Dive composition (frame icons by role)
- [04:44] [T] Lineup: XP laners who can jump into the enemy backline; assassin junglers (or ones that can dive in); high-mobility mid laners; roamers that can jump in; agile marksmen. (Frame labels: XP lane "Can jump into the enemy backline", jungle "Ones that can dive in", mid "High mobility", roam "Can jump in", gold "Agile Marksmen".)
- [05:09] [T] Goal: dive into the enemy backline during teamfights and eliminate the enemy DAMAGE DEALERS first. Win condition: whenever a fight breaks out, the enemy damage dealers must go down first.
- [05:23] [T] Strong enough that MPL teams use it.
- [05:27] [T] Weakness: countered by heroes that can cancel engage gauges (specific names in LIVE table). Ban them.
- [05:35] [T] Dive needs tight coordination: focus on ONE target at a time; don't waste skills on the same enemy; use just enough to secure the kill, then move to the next target.

### Comp 2: Poke composition (opposite of dive)
- [06:03] [T] Structure (frame): 3 poke heroes + 1 executor + 1 tanky disengager who can stop enemy engages.
- [06:13] [T] Role constraints: XP laner and jungler can be poke, executor or tank heroes; mid and gold lane MUST be poke heroes; roamers should be a tank or a poke hero.
- [06:24] [T] Goal: chip enemies from long range, then the executor finishes them once HP is low.
- [06:32] [T] Win condition: the enemy team must already be LOW HP before the fight starts.
- [06:38] [T] Risk: if the enemy dives first, your tank must cancel their engage immediately.
- [06:44] [T] Poke heroes need spamable long-range skills.
- [07:00] [T] Executors must have skills that reset on kill.
- [07:18] [T] Disengager = tanky hero that can stop the enemy engage (jungle/XP/roam options listed in LIVE table).
- [07:18] [T] Rank caveat: poke comps work great below Mythic Glory (most players there do not know how to counter it); in higher ranks (frame "Above" Mythic Glory) players understand vision and flanking, so this comp falls off.

### Comp 3: Front-to-back composition
- [07:32] [T] Works in every rank; "most common comp", used by casuals, elo boosters and MPL teams.
- [07:47] [T] Two parts: BACKLINE and FRONTLINE.
- [07:49] [T] Backline = TWO consistent damage dealers with DIFFERENT damage types (one physical, one magic; frame shows physical/magic icons). Consistent, NOT burst. Gold lane: every marksman except one named exception (LIVE table); mid lane: DPS mages (examples in LIVE table).
- [08:08] [T] Frontline = ONE initiator, ONE sustained hero, ONE disengager (frame shows three frontline heroes). XP lane, jungle and roam can fill these (examples in LIVE table).
- [08:24] [T] Win condition: your backline must melt the enemy's front line, while your front line ZONES their back line to keep it out of the fight.
- [08:33] [T] Usually the XP laner or roamer handles zoning with lots of CC.
- [08:40] [T] This explains the earlier MPL clip: that team focused tanks because it plays front-to-back (hit the front line first).
- [08:47] [T] Weaknesses: relies too much on marksman and mage (if they get picked off the team can do nothing); depends on 5-man fights, so it is weak against split push.

### Comp 4: Hypercarry composition
- [09:04] [T] Goal: make ONE player a star who carries the team. Wins games fast.
- [09:14] [T] Hypercarry can be gold lane or jungle (frame icons: 3 gold laners, 2 junglers).
- [09:23] [T] Only high-DPS heroes can be hypercarries, NOT burst heroes with long cooldowns.
- [09:31] [T] The roamer always sticks with the hypercarry; the rest of the team must be independent.
- [09:38] [T] Win condition: the hypercarry must get rich (gold). If they fall behind in gold you will certainly lose.
- [09:46] [T] Risk: relying on one hero; reward: if they get ahead you snowball hard and end fast.

### Comp comparison
- [09:54] [T] Every team comp has strengths, weaknesses and win conditions; none hard-counters another; "it's all about execution."

### Pick order / priority list
- [10:05] [T] Pick order matters as much as understanding comps. After bans, decide comp; every pick must fit it.
- [10:24] [T] Priority list (frame "Priority List"): 1. OP heroes. 2. Flexible heroes who can play multiple lanes. 3. Counter-picks. 4. Tanks. 5. Damage dealers. 6. Initiators.
- [10:26] [T] Flexible heroes = those who can play multiple lanes (examples roamer/jungler, gold/mid in the LIVE table). A flexible pick keeps lane assignment open so you can adapt to enemy picks.
- [10:56] [T] Method: go down the list; if nothing OP or flexible that fits your comp remains, move to counter-picks; then tanks (to complete the frontline), then damage dealers after seeing the enemy picks, then initiators.
- [11:26] [T] When picking a damage dealer, choose the damage type your team lacks: a magic gold laner is more suitable if the team already has a lot of physical damage.
- [11:32] [T] If the enemy picks a hero that counters your flexible pick, change the lane/role of the flexible hero (e.g. move it to a diving roamer role) instead of dropping the comp.
- [11:00] [T] Use the comp requirement list as a checklist while drafting (frame for dive: XP lane "Can Dive", roam "Can Dive", jungle "Diving or Assassin", mid "Agile", gold "Agile").

### Adapting to trio, duo, solo
- [12:14] [T] Trio: focus on the MID TRIO (mid, jungle, roam); they dictate rotations and follow the same 4 comp positions.
- [12:25] [T] Duo: depends on your teammates; you can be paired with another duo, a trio, or 3 solos (frame: duo + (duo + solo / solo + solo + solo)).
- [12:36] [T] If paired with a trio: follow their draft direction and fit into their comp.
- [12:42] [T] Otherwise focus on 3 synergies (frame icons: roam+gold, mid+jungle, jungle+roam).

Synergy 1: Roam + gold lane
- [12:47] [T] Check your team's damage type. If the team already has 2 physical heroes, pick a magic gold laner.
- [12:57] [T] If you are the roamer: look at your team's front line; if you have no tank, you must pick one, unless you run a roamer+gold combo that covers it (the video's examples are in the LIVE table).
- [13:09] [T] Make your roamer cover your gold laner's weaknesses: e.g. a gold laner with high damage but no mobility ("once slowed, she's stuck") needs a roamer with CC immunity and a trap skill so enemies can't escape her.
- [13:30] [T] It works both ways: a gold laner can cover the roamer's weakness, e.g. a roamer whose hook is deadly but easy to dodge benefits from a gold laner with a CC skill that makes the hook easy to land.

Synergy 2: Mid + jungle (video calls it the strongest duo)
- [13:51] [T] Strongest duo is mid + jungle. Two rules (frame): 1. Complement each other. 2. Pay attention (to their behavior).
- [14:01] [T] Behavior rule: if your jungler desperately needs to farm, do NOT pick farm-heavy mages, and vice versa.
- [14:12] [T] If your mage needs to farm a lot, pick a jungler that can PRESSURE the enemy mid (examples in LIVE table).

Synergy 3: Jungle + roam
- [14:21] [T] Works similarly to mid + jungle.

### Solo rules ("lower your expectations")
- [14:27] [T] If solo you must lower your expectations; just follow these principles (frame "Solo"):
  1. Your team must have both a frontline and backline.
  2. You must have both physical and magic damage dealers.
  3. You must have both early and late game heroes.
  4. You have someone who can engage and someone who can disengage.
- [14:49] [T] If matched with a trio, follow their composition instead.

### Hero-pool mastery per role (how many and what type)
- [14:54] [T] To draft well, master a small pool of archetypes per role (frames show 3 slots each; specific heroes in LIVE table):
  - Roam: 1 initiator tank, 1 dive tank, 1 support.
  - Jungle: 1 tank jungler, 1 magic assassin, 1 physical assassin.
  - XP lane: 1 sustain, 1 damage, 1 tank (frame; the audio is garbled here, see Uncertain).
  - Gold lane: 1 late-game carry, 1 strong laner, 1 magic marksman.
  - Mid lane: 1 late-game mage, 1 burst mage, 1 utility mage.
- [15:54] [T] Above all, know every hero's skills and passives so you can counter the enemy's gameplay.

## Terms & definitions
- [00:53] [T] Draft ban (MPL): turn-based; ban, pick, ban, pick.
- [00:58] [T] Blind ban (public): all bans first, then picks.
- [01:50] [T] Team comp: team composition; the kind of lineup your team has.
- [02:12] [T] Win comp / win condition: the specific condition your team must fulfill to win.
- [02:25] [T] OP hero: overpowered hero (usually ~3 per meta in the video's model).
- [05:00] [T] Dive comp: engage into enemy backline to kill damage dealers first.
- [06:05] [T] Poke comp: 3 poke + 1 executor + 1 tanky disengager; chip then finish.
- [07:00] [T] Executor: hero whose skills reset on kill; finishes low-HP enemies in poke comp.
- [07:49] [T] Front-to-back comp: backline = 2 consistent damage dealers (different damage types); frontline = initiator + sustained hero + disengager.
- [09:14] [T] Hypercarry comp: one high-DPS (non-burst) carry in gold lane or jungle, roamer sticks with him, the rest independent.
- [07:49] [T] Consistent damage dealer: sustained DPS, not burst.
- [08:33] [T] Zoning: front line uses CC to keep the enemy backline out of the fight.
- [05:27] [T] Engage-gauge cancel: heroes that cancel the engage (specific names in LIVE table).
- [14:21] [T] Pressure the enemy mid: jungler role that helps a farm-hungry mage.

## Worked examples
- Ban phase (10:43). Our team bans three heroes (listed in LIVE table), enemy bans three; several strong heroes remain open. The OP front-to-back heroes (e.g. two the enemy banned) are gone, so the video chooses the dive comp. Needs: dive-capable XP and roam, diving/assassin jungler, agile mid and gold. Extra bans (the last two) go on the heroes that cover dive's weakness (engage cancel). [10:43] [T]
- Pick phase (10:54). Follow the priority list: (1) first pick a strong flexible hero; (2) the enemy picks a hero that counters it, so the flexible hero is switched to the roam (diving roamer) role; (3) nothing OP/flexible that fits dive remains, so go to counter-picks: pick a jungler that counters the enemy's XP hero and fits dive; (4) pick a tanky diving XP laner to complete the frontline; (5) after enemy picks, pick damage dealers: a magic gold laner (because team already has lots of physical damage from jungler and XP laner) and a mid laner. Result: dive vs front-to-back. [10:54] [T]
- Roam+gold synergy (12:47). Team has 2 physical heroes: gold laner picks magic damage. Roamer with no tank on team: must pick a tank unless a combo that already covers it. [12:47] [T]
- Gold laner weakness cover (13:09). High-damage gold laner with no mobility is stuck once slowed; a roamer with CC immunity and a trapping skill fixes that. [13:09] [T]
- Roamer weakness cover (13:30). Roamer whose hook is easy to dodge is paired with a gold laner whose skill CCs the enemy, so the hook lands. [13:30] [T]
- Mid + jungle (14:01). Farm-needy jungler + farm-heavy mage = bad (both starve); instead pair a farm-needy mage with a pressuring jungler. [14:01] [T]
