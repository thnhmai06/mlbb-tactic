# Is Your Macro Good? Let's test it
Source: Is Your Macro Good Let’s test it.mp4 | ~11:53 | Four-scenario macro quiz (easy to hard) from real MLBB footage; thesis: macro = information -> decision -> consequence, not memorised rules.

## Summary
- Macro is "decision making under uncertainty": a few seconds to read the map, understand what happened, and pick the option giving the team the best outcome. The best option is often not the obvious one. [00:00] [T]
- Core question to ask every time: "Based on the information I have, what is the best thing I can do?" [11:16] [T]
- Four scenarios: (1) laner, enemy mid disappears; (2) jungler, enemy invades your blue buff; (3) roamer, marksman about to be jumped by 3 while turtle is up; (4) read the fight state: who is winning (ult wasted). [00:58] [T]
- Closing framework: when ahead pick the good option with the most value for least risk; when behind lose less and force the enemy to make the next mistake. [09:18] [T]

## Tactical knowledge

### General philosophy
- Macro is not a list of rules; you can know all the rules and still not know what to do when the situation happens. [00:00] [T]
- Information does not tell you exactly what WILL happen, it tells you what COULD happen. Good macro = adjusting your play BEFORE you find out what actually happens. [02:53] [T]
- You do not need to know exactly what the enemy will do; you only need to recognise what could happen from the information you have, then adjust positioning. [02:30] [T]
- You cannot know where every enemy is, cannot know which decision they will make, and cannot control your teammates. You can control how you react to the information you have. [10:46] [T]
- Cycle: information -> decision -> consequence; every decision creates a new situation, which creates another decision. Repeat all game; the better you understand that chain, the easier the game is to control and predict. [11:16] [T]
- Do not memorise every rule; ask the one question: "based on the information I have, what is the best thing I can do?" That is playing the game instead of just reacting to it. [11:16] [T]
- Applies to every role (gold, exp, roam, jungle, mid); the game constantly gives information, the question is what you do with it. [00:29] [T]

### Scenario 1: laner, enemy mid disappears (see Worked examples)
- Rule: when an enemy (mid laner) vanishes from the map and your roamer is not nearby, assume a gank is possible; it takes the enemy several seconds to reach your lane, so you have time to use the information. [01:27] [T]
- Rule: adjust position and keep farming safely (not "stand in the middle of the lane pretending nothing happened"). [01:27] [T]
- If the gank never comes you lost nothing; if it comes you are already positioned to react. [02:53] [T]
- Anti-pattern: keep playing exactly the same way, get caught, then type "no roam, no gang" and blame the team. You had the information (enemy mid gone, roamer not nearby) and put yourself where you could be trapped. [01:58] [T]
- Over-reaction (giving up the wave completely) is also wrong: it is safe but gives up resources you did not have to give up. [02:30] [T]

### Scenario 2: jungler, enemy invades your blue buff
- Rule: if the enemy already controls the area (invade on your buff, no allies near), do not try to recover the lost resource; take what is still available: go to the opposite side of the map, clear your camps, take whatever resources you can. [04:24] [T]
- Principle: do not give the enemy a second advantage on top of the one they already created. Sometimes the best play is not recovering what you lost but recognising it is lost and not losing anything else. [04:32] [T]
- Buff importance does not justify walking into a losing situation: "just because the buff is important" is not a reason to fight for it. [03:32] [T]
- Why not retake the buff alone: if you miss your Retribution or get caught you lose the fight, the buff, your tempo and potentially another jungle camp; one lost resource becomes a bigger problem. [03:42] [T]
- Why not fight first: forcing them away only works with teammates around; with no allies nearby you just go in to die, even if ahead in gold. [03:56] [T]
- Why not ping and wait: better than blindly going in, but teammates will not arrive in time even if they notice; waiting lets one lost blue buff stop your whole tempo and jungle rotation pace. [04:10] [T]

### Scenario 3: roamer, marksman jumped by 3, turtle up on XP side
- Rule: when the enemy commits several players to one place (e.g. 3 on your gold lane), ask "what did their play leave vulnerable?" instead of "how do I stop it / save my marksman?". Find who is missing from the commit and punish that. [05:41] [T]
- Here the missing one is the enemy jungler = the biggest unknown on the map; track him down. [05:53] [T]
- Roamer's job is not to save teammates from every situation; if you will arrive too late, ping the teammate to retreat and let them handle it; sometimes accept the loss and find a better trade. [06:09] [T]
- Evaluate each option as a TRADE (what you lose vs what you gain), not in isolation. [06:39] [T]
- Why the answer is better: enemy jungler is likely taking his buff before the turtle; the rest of his team is committed on gold lane so you can catch him alone; killing him lets your jungler invade their buff, take their jungle resources, and you still move to the turtle and take it because their jungler is dead. [06:59] [T]
- Resulting trade: you lose marksman and maybe gold-lane tower; you gain enemy jungler + their buff + turtle = much better. [07:27] [T]
- 5v5 game: you can't always react to what the enemy is doing; sometimes the correct response is not to stop their play but to find the best thing you can do BECAUSE of their play. Take the information the enemy gave you and turn it into your own advantage. [07:36] [T]

### Scenario 4: read the fight state (who is winning)
- Rule: scoreboard (gold, kills, area control) does not tell you what just happened; read the current fight state, especially which key abilities/ultimates are on cooldown. [08:29] [T]
- Rule: when an enemy key tool (ult) has just been used and hit nothing, the team that still has its tools is favoured; the window is now. [08:44] [T]
- A team does not need to become stronger than the opponent; it only needs to recognise that the opponent's key ability is gone and use that window. [09:00] [T]
- Sometimes the best macro play is simply to wait for your abilities to come back and make the enemy fight you when you are actually ready. [09:00] [T]
- Being ahead and controlling the objective area does not make you favoured if your key tool is on cooldown and you are overextended. [08:44] [T]

### Playing from ahead vs behind
- Ahead: you usually have several good options (take an objective, invade, push a side lane, reset and prepare for the next play). The question is not "what's correct" but "which good option gives the most value with the least risk". [09:18] [T]
- Ahead: don't throw the advantage by forcing something. [10:17] [T]
- Behind: you usually have no great option; you choose between a bad option, a worse option, and one that could lose the game. [09:47] [T]
- Behind: the goal is not simply to win the game; it is to create a situation where the enemy can make their next mistake. [10:00] [T]
- Behind examples given: give up an objective to take a tower; give up a jungle to gang the opposite side; stop contesting a fight you know you can't win and wait for the enemy to overextend. [10:00] [T]
- Behind: you are not trying to magically turn the game in five seconds, you are trying to lose less; keep making the least bad decision until the enemy has to make a decision too, then the game can turn. Don't throw the game trying to get the advantage back instantly. [10:17] [T]

## Terms & definitions
- Macro: decision making under uncertainty, not a list of rules; reading map information and choosing the best outcome for the team. [00:00] [T]
- Information: tells you what could happen, not what will. [02:53] [T]
- Window (as used in scenario 4): the moment the opponent's key ability is on cooldown; the team that has tools available acts. [09:00] [T]
- Loop: information -> decision -> consequence. [11:16] [T]

## Worked examples

Each scenario was shown from real gameplay (a 2x2 option screen "QUESTION" with a countdown timer), then answered.

### Scenario 1 (easy): laner, enemy mid disappears
- Situation shown: you play lane (gold lane per the video), clearing your wave, healthy, everything normal; gameplay clock ~01:05-01:14, score 1-0 (blue), your hero with wave minions near a bush edge. Minimap (viewed at 01:40 frame) shows the enemy mid laner no longer visible/near; your roamer is busy elsewhere. [01:27] [T]
- Options: A keep farming normally; B play aggressively to kill the enemy marksman; C back off and give up the wave completely; D play the next few seconds safely. [00:58] [T]
- Correct: D. [01:27] [T]
- Reasoning: enemy mid vanished so a gank is possible, but he needs several seconds to reach you, so you have time to adjust positioning. A = same as ignoring the info and getting caught (then blaming "no roam, no gang"); B = very similar bad outcome to A; C = safe but gives up resources unnecessarily. D costs nothing if he never comes and puts you in position to react if he does. [01:27] [T]

### Scenario 2 (medium): jungler, enemy invaded your blue buff
- Situation shown: you are the jungler (Fanny per the video, relies heavily on blue buff), clock ~01:31, score 1-0; screen shows "Jungle Invaded" alert and an enemy fighting you at your buff camp; no allies around; Retribution available. [03:08] [T]
- Options: A try to play safe and retri (Retribution-steal) your blue buff; B fight the enemy first and try to force them away; C ping your teammate and wait for them to help; D continue farming on the opposite side of the map. [03:13] [T]
- Correct: D. [03:23] [T]
- Reasoning: A) might get lucky and steal it, but missing Retribution or getting caught loses fight + buff + tempo + maybe another camp. B) works only with teammates around; none here, so you just die even if ahead in gold. C) better than blind but the team won't arrive in time and you stall your tempo/rotation. D) the enemy already controls the area; take what is still available on the other side and don't give another advantage. [03:42] [T]
- Lesson: sometimes the best play is recognising you already lost the resource and making sure you lose nothing else. [04:45] [T]

### Scenario 3 (hard): roamer in mid lane, marksman about to be jumped, turtle up on XP side
- Situation shown: you are the roamer in mid lane; turtle is up on the XP (exp) side of the map (minimap shows the turtle on the exp-side river); clock ~02:56 in the shown frame; the enemy jumps your marksman on the other side (gold lane) with three people. Diagram (frame 06:35-06:50): three enemy heroes clustered at the top-left (your gold-lane side), your remaining team (blue, 2-3 heroes) at the bottom-right near the turtle; red arrow shows the enemy would rotate from their gold-lane commit toward the turtle. Minimap diagram (frame 05:30) marks the missing enemy (enemy jungler) with question marks. [04:55] [T]
- Options: A go gold lane and try to save your marksman; B prepare for the turtle; C track down the enemy jungler; D gank the XP lane instead. [04:59] [T]
- Correct: C (B, C and D are "all kind of correct in their own terms", but C is best). [05:11] [T]
- Reasoning by option: [05:30] [T]
  - A: not bad in a normal situation, but you are too late; you arrive to an already lost fight. Better: ping the marksman to retreat and let them handle it.
  - B: reasonable (most of the enemy is on gold lane so you could take the turtle quickly) but the trade is bad: lose marksman, maybe gold-lane tower, gain only one turtle.
  - D: could kill the exp laner before he rotates, but after that kill the rest of the enemy can still rotate to the turtle and their jungler is alive; you are only hoping the kill compensates for everything elsewhere; possibility, not guaranteed.
  - C: enemy jungler is probably taking his buff before the turtle, alone because the rest of the team is on gold lane. Kill him -> your jungler invades their buff, you take their jungle resource, then take the turtle since their jungler is dead.
- Net trade for C: lose marksman (+ maybe gold-lane tower), gain enemy jungler + his buff + turtle. [07:27] [T]
- Lesson: ask "what did they leave vulnerable?" not "how do I save my marksman?". [07:36] [T]

### Scenario 4 (final): who is favoured? (fight-state reading)
- Situation shown: pro match, MPL MY Season 18 (regular season, match 1 game 1, best of 3), "Late phase", clock ~17:14-17:36. Team Rey (TR, blue/left side) vs AC Esports (AC, gold/right side). Scoreboard at 17:24-17:25: kills 9 (Team Rey) vs 7 (AC); gold 54.2K vs 52.6K, Team Rey +1.5K; objective icons shown 2 / 1 / 7 for Team Rey vs 5 / 1 / 1 for AC (icon meanings not identified on screen). Both teams alive around the Lord; blue is ahead in gold and controls the area around the Lord. [08:10] [T]
- Options: A Team Blue; B Team Red; C completely even. [08:20] [T]
- Correct: B, Team Red (surprising; "if you pick blue I don't blame you"). [08:29] [T]
- Reasoning: the scoreboard does not show what just happened and the fight state just changed. Blue's hero "Barrage" (as transcribed) used his ultimate and got nothing from it, so one of blue's important tools is on cooldown while red still has theirs and recognises the gap immediately. A red hero ("Esmeralda") finds an opening and jumps onto blue while overextended; "Brody" (a blue hero) gets killed; blue is forced to retreat, having lost one of its strongest assets and the important tool from the earlier ult. Frame after the fight (17:32): kills 9-8, Team Rey lead down to +818 gold. [08:44] [T]
- Lesson: red did not need to become stronger, only to notice the key ability was gone: "this is our window". Sometimes best macro = wait for your abilities to come back and make the enemy fight you when you are ready. [09:00] [T]
