- [00:30] [T] What decides damage type is the skill itself, not the item you buy. Example given: a hero who buys magic items can still deal physical damage, because his skill deals physical damage (on-screen: X.Borg's skill text with "Physical Damage" in red and "Magic Damage" in blue; its scaling reads "+60% Total Physical Attack" and "+40% Total Magic Power").
- [01:40] [T] Item choice rule: look at how your skills scale, not the type of damage they deal. If the scaling color is blue, buy magic items. If the color is red, buy physical items. (Bottom-line: "read, read and read again" the skill description.)

- [02:10] [T] Spacing: every hero has a different range; exploit it so you hit the enemy without being hit back (on-screen: two heroes with overlapping range circles; the one with the longer circle pokes, the other has to close in).

### High level: hidden mechanics (the "?" pillar)
- [04:10] [T] Mid-level mechanics come with experience; high-level mechanics are harder and full of hidden mechanics.

#### Animation cancel
- [04:10] [T] Example: Gatot Kaccha (Gatotkaca) - the one on the right deals more damage than the one on the left. Reason: animation cancel.
- [04:25] [T] His first skill animation is: jumps, lands (then ends). If you cast his first skill again in the middle of the jump (in the air), the next cast starts immediately and the damage comes out faster. Without cancel: Jumps -> Lands -> end. With cancel: Jumps -> end (the landing is skipped) -> next cast. Net effect: damage comes out faster.

#### Basic attack reset (basic attack cancel)
- [04:46] [T] Example: Clint - his basic attack animation is: aims, shoots, pulls back; the "pull back" part is wasted time.
- [05:03] [T] Rule: use a skill right after Clint fires; the skill's animation cancels the backward movement (pull back), so the next basic attacks come out faster. On-screen: sequence "Aims - Shoots - Pulls back" repeated; with cancel the "Pulls back" part is removed and attacks repeat faster than without.

#### Curving a hook (Franco)
- [05:15] [T] After Franco hooks, press flicker; the hook moves with the hero (it is re-positioned by the flicker). The trick works in any direction.

#### Input buffer (Tip 4/4)
- [05:29] [T] Question: Alt first then flicker, or flicker first then alt (Chou)? Answer is input buffer. "Input buffer" is not a buff.
- [05:42] [T] Analogy: you want to splash water on a streamer friend from close range; open the bottle first, then walk up, so you splash the moment you are close enough (Option 1).
- [06:12] [T] Chou's alt needs a nearby enemy to activate. If there is no target, the alt is held in the input buffer for a moment and activates the moment the condition is met.
- [06:25] [T] So: press alt first (it waits in the buffer), then flicker toward the enemy; once Chou arrives near the enemy, the alt fires instantly. Therefore the link done this way is faster than flicker-then-alt. That is why the answer is always alt -> flicker (on-screen: alt icon -> flicker icon).
- [06:25] [T] General idea: if an ability has a condition (e.g. needs a nearby target), queue it before the movement/blink that satisfies the condition so it fires with no delay.

## Terms & definitions
- [00:30] Physical Attack / Magic Power: stats that boost your damage.
- [00:30] Physical Damage / Magic Damage: the damage actually dealt; type is set by the skill.
- [01:10] Last hit: landing the final hit on a minion (gold) while denying the enemy the same chance.
- [02:10] Spacing: using the difference in hero ranges to hit the enemy without being hit back.
- [02:55] Power spike: when your hero is at their strongest and most impactful.
- [04:10] Animation cancel: casting a skill again during the first part of an animation (e.g. mid-air) to skip the rest of the animation so damage comes out faster.
- [04:46] Basic attack reset: using a skill right after a basic attack to cancel the pull-back animation, speeding up attacks.
- [05:15] Curving a hook: using flicker after a hook to move the hook with the hero.
- [05:29] Input buffer: a short window in which a pressed ability waits if its activation condition (e.g. nearby enemy) is not met yet, then fires instantly once met.
