# I Studied Every Item Combo So You Don't Have To
Source: I Studied Every Item Combo So You Don't Have To.mp4 | ~7:34 | Survey of the "best" MLBB item combinations, grouped as basic (same-type items: physical / magic / defense) and advanced (mixed item types), with the problem each combo solves and which hero archetypes should use it.

## Summary
- Combos are chosen to solve one concrete problem each: burst, tanky enemies, on-hit damage for attack-speed marksmen, survivability under burst, split-push mobility, sustain + damage for magic fighters, % HP burst for mages, super-tank durability, damage-dealing tanks, enemy crowd control, healing amplification, and kiting/slowing.
- The video's rule of thumb: a combo is good because the two items' passives/stats feed each other (e.g. a defensive stat that keeps you alive longer = more time to deal damage), not because each is strong alone.
- Each combo comes with a hero-archetype restriction ("who should / should not build it"). Several combos explicitly do NOT work for certain archetypes (HP-building heroes, tanks, etc.).
- The video also notes counters (a combo that beats another combo, and one item that counters the all-HP tank triple).
- Structure on screen: "Tips #n/11" cards, plus "Versus #1/2 and #2/2" cards (which items to pair depending on hero type) and a "Basic Combos" overview grid (Physical / Magic / Defense).
- Note: intro says "13 best combinations" but the on-screen counter is 11 tips; the extra combos are shown as sub-combos (see Uncertain).

## Tactical knowledge

### How the video frames item combos
- [00:00] [T] Combos are judged by effect on a specific problem (burst, anti-tank, anti-burst, anti-CC, sustain), and the video repeatedly attaches each to a hero archetype it fits.
- [05:54] [T] "Basic combos" = combos made of items of the same type (physical, magic, or defense). "Advanced combos" = combos that mix several item types (e.g. boots + armor-type item, or a lifesteal source + a heal-amplifier).
- [05:54] [T] Basic-combo grid shown at the end of the basic section: Physical (5 combos: C1, C2, C3, C4, C5), Magic (2 combos: C6, C7), Defense (2 combos: C8, C9).

### Physical, burst (C1)
- [00:00] [T] Pair an item that adds extra (bonus) damage with an item that adds true damage to build a burst ("one shot") combo. Extra damage + true damage together = a very high burst on the first hit.
- [00:30] [T] Best for burst-style heroes (assassin/burst archetypes).
- [00:45] [T] Weakness: against enemies with a lot of physical defense this combo struggles to hurt them. The fix is C2 (anti-tank penetration + % HP damage).

### Anti-tank (C2)
- [00:45] [T] Problem solved: enemy has high defense/HP (tank). Solution = one item that gives penetration (pick one of two penetration options) + one item that adds extra damage to heroes with higher max HP. Combined, "the enemy tank will be way less tanky".
- [00:55] [T] Use it for physical damage dealers: assassins and marksmen ("rather for physical damage dealers").
- [01:10] [T] Do NOT use for heroes that themselves buy HP items (the card says "Can't be used for heroes that buy HP items"). The video gives no reason; do not infer one.

### Attack-speed marksman on-hit combo (C3)
- [01:15] [T] Rule: every attack-speed marksman should build this three-item combo (the video says "must build").
- [01:15] [T] Logic: two items deal extra damage on hit; the third item's passive makes their on-hit passives activate three times on every third basic attack ("DHS and Corrosion's passive will be activated 3x on every third hit" as on-screen text). Result = big extra on-hit damage burst on every third attack.
- [01:35] [T] Intended for attack-speed marksmen (hero examples in the table).

### Anti-burst: lifesteal + hybrid defense (C4)
- [01:55] [T] Principle: when you play marksman (or a fragile carry) you are "the easiest target to burst" so you must buy a defensive answer; the answer is a lifesteal-converting item plus a lifesteal source.
- [02:00] [T] Logic: the defensive item converts your lifesteal into hybrid defense, so the more lifesteal you stack the harder you are to kill. The conversion is better for fighters than for other roles (rate in table).
- [02:15] [T] Pairing rule (card "Versus #1/2"): pair the defensive item with a critical-damage item if you play a crit hero, and with an on-hit/attack-speed item if you play an attack-speed hero.
- [02:25] [T] Tip #3/11: even if all item slots are full you can still buy a consumable that gives extra lifesteal (shown with 6 full slots; item named in the table).
- [02:30] [T] At low HP the defensive item gives a shield that buys you time to lifesteal, so you survive the burst window and heal back.

### Split-push / rotation for attack-speed heroes (C5)
- [02:38] [T] Problem: attack-speed heroes that need movement speed to split push or rotate.
- [02:54] [T] The movement-speed item's passive does NOT rely on critical hits (unlike the two crit-reliant items shown on screen), so it is compatible with a crit-to-attack-speed converter.
- [03:02] [T] Key idea: the converter item sets your displayed crit chance to 0%, but crit chance is not lost. It is transformed into attack speed. With more attack speed, the movement-speed item's cooldown is shorter, so it procs more often.
- [03:20] [T] Use for attack-speed heroes (examples in table).

### Magic fighters: sustain + damage (C6)
- [03:28] [T] All earlier combos are for physical heroes; for magic users there are two combos.
- [03:36] [T] C6 is for magic fighters that want to deal damage in battle AND sustain in fights.
- [03:44] [T] Logic: one item gives hybrid defense that stacks each time you deal magic damage; the other gives hybrid lifesteal + extra magic damage that stacks. Each makes you last longer, and lasting longer means dealing more damage (positive feedback). Both stack counters can be filled at the same time.
- [03:56] [T] Card: "Hybrid defense + lifesteal".

### Damage mages vs high HP (C7)
- [04:15] [T] Used by damage-dealer mages.
- [04:23] [T] The first item gives extra magic damage if you deal at least a threshold of magic damage (on-screen: "at least 800 magic damage"); that extra damage scales with the enemy's HP (screen label: "Anti Thick HP"). The second item provides anti magic defense ("Anti Magic Def").
- [04:39] [T] Combined effect: "the enemy tank will literally melt". The counter to this is C8 (see below).

### Super tank (C8)
- [04:45] [T] Tank can survive C7 if they build C8: one item reduces incoming damage when you are above a threshold (shown in transcript as "over 500"), with damage reduction that scales with HP; the other item gives a lot of HP. Result = "very hard to burst".
- [05:01] [T] Use for pure tanks that build HP items.

### Damage tank (C9) and the all-HP warning
- [05:17] [T] If you want a tank that deals damage, pair the HP item from C8 with a different helmet-type item that deals damage to surroundings scaling with your HP.
- [05:24] [T] This lets damage tanks "clear camps quickly" (jungle clear).
- [05:32] [T] Tip #7/11: a hero can build all three HP items together (the 1200 HP + 1550 HP + 1200 HP triple) for "tanky + damage", BUT you need to be careful: this triple is easily countered by one specific item (shown with a "Counter:" card).
- [05:40] [T] Reason for the counter is not explained by the video; do not infer one.

### Advanced: anti-CC (C10) and sustain fighter extension (C11 = C10 + a spell-vamp item)
- [06:03] [T] Anti-CC combo: two items each give crowd-control reduction (30% and 15% on screen; stacks into more). Any hero can use it to counter heroes with high CC.

- [06:26] [T] For fighters that are prone to crowd control and need sustain: add a third item (spell-vamp item). The CC-reducing armor and the third item both have passives that stack with time in combat, so you are stronger the longer you stay, and the spell vamp keeps you in the fight even longer.
- [06:36] [T] Tip #9/11 (screen): the two passives raise stats during battle (before battle vs during battle values in the table). The core logic: both items scale up inside a fight.

### Advanced: sustain amplifier (C12)
- [06:55] [T] Healing amplification rule: any lifesteal or spell-vamp item combines with a heal-amplifier item, because the amplifier "will increase any heal that you receive".
- [07:02] [T] Best for sustain heroes that rely on spell vamp or lifesteal.

## Worked examples
- [02:54] [T] Situation: you are an attack-speed hero who needs mobility to split push or rotate; you want the movement-speed item but also the crit-to-attack-speed converter. Decision: build both. Reason: the movement-speed passive does not need crits, and the converter turns crit chance into attack speed rather than deleting it, which shortens the movement-speed item's cooldown.
- [02:00] [T] Situation: you play marksman and keep dying to burst. Decision: build the lifesteal-to-hybrid-defense item plus a lifesteal source; at low HP its shield gives you time to heal. If slots are full, buy the lifesteal consumable. Reason: every 1% lifesteal turns into hybrid defense, so more lifesteal = more defense.
- [00:45] [T] Situation: your burst combo (C1) is not hurting a high-defense enemy tank. Decision: switch to the anti-tank combo (C2): penetration + extra damage vs high-max-HP heroes. Reason: C1 relies on raw damage that defense mitigates.
- [04:39] [T] Situation: you are a damage mage facing an enemy tank. Decision: C7 melts the tank because the lantern-type item scales with enemy HP and the second item provides anti magic defense. Counter-situation: if the tank builds C8, C7 is much less effective.
- [06:26] [T] Situation: you are a fighter prone to crowd control and need sustain. Decision: anti-CC combo (C10) + the spell-vamp item (C11). Reason: both passives stack as the fight goes on, and spell vamp extends the fight.
